# Art Direction

**Status:** DECIDED direction; final assets PROVISIONAL.

## Core language

2D illustrated storybook/coloring-book world with strong clean line work, warm handcrafted shapes, child-friendly detail and large readable silhouettes.

Add a light manga/anime influence in expressions, staging, movement and character appeal without turning the product into a direct imitation of any named work, artist or studio.

## Original visual attributes

Translate mood references into original attributes rather than copied style:

- cozy coloring-book warmth;
- rounded organic contours;
- confident variable line weight;
- many enclosed/colorable shapes where practical;
- expressive eyes/eyebrows/body gesture;
- rich but readable environmental micro-details;
- playful object silhouettes;
- clear foreground/midground/background depth;
- small discoveries that reward looking around;
- scene composition that remains readable on landscape tablet.

A schematic drawing made mostly from primitive circles, straight lines and generic icons is acceptable only as temporary technical scaffolding. It is not the reviewed art target after Phase 1B.

## Scene richness

Primary game scenes should not feel empty.

Overworld/Board concept art should normally contain:

- foreground framing details;
- a readable midground route/activity area;
- background landmarks/atmosphere;
- varied terrain/object scale;
- texture/value detail that survives stolen-color mode;
- multiple non-interactive small scenic details;
- controlled negative space around active interactions.

Richness must support exploration without making challenge areas visually noisy.

## Character standard

Maittê and companions should look like inhabitants of the same illustrated world, not SVG icons placed over scenery.

Character requirements are further specified in `CHARACTER-ART.md` and `MAITTE.md`.

At challenge scale, faces/poses must communicate speaker, attention, thought, success and retry states clearly.

## Color as system

The world begins largely uncolored/line-art after the theft. Color restoration is a game-state visualization. Completed areas progressively fill with color while untouched areas preserve the stolen-color state.

The visual system must support partial restoration at:

- Maittê/avatar level;
- local board/landmark level;
- subject-world representation in the Overworld.

Stolen-color is not flat gray UI. It should remain attractive through warm paper values, ink hierarchy, texture, hatching/value and depth cues.

## UI integration

Avoid floating corporate/dashboard cards as the dominant visual language. Controls should feel embedded in the illustrated world.

Text remains readable and accessible; diegetic UI must not sacrifice usability.

Challenge content should emerge from world objects/characters where feasible instead of looking like a generic web form placed over scenery.

## Animation

Prioritize meaningful animation:

- world zoom;
- Maittê movement/acting;
- color flowing/restoring;
- character reactions;
- companion speaking/entrances;
- puzzle selection/place feedback;
- success/retry feedback.

Ambient motion is secondary and must reduce during focused learning.

## Transition language

- Overworld → world: spatial zoom.
- Major narrative/context transition: page-turn/book metaphor when appropriate.
- Puzzle entry/exit: preserve spatial continuity with the board rather than feel like navigating to an unrelated web form.

## Reference policy

Mood references discussed during ideation are directional only. Final characters, environments and assets must be original.

Do not ask an implementation/image agent to reproduce the exact style of a named living artist, studio, anime, manga or game. Instead describe the approved visual attributes above.

## Phase 1B acceptance bar

The current demonstrated Overworld, Dunas Douradas and Challenge Stage should materially exceed Phase 1A schematic quality by showing:

1. richer environmental layering/detail;
2. organic hand-drawn-feeling silhouettes;
3. concept-quality Maittê character art;
4. at least one concept-quality companion;
5. expressive character acting;
6. a Challenge Stage that reads as part of the adventure rather than a web form;
7. clear stolen/restored visual richness without relying on percentage labels.
