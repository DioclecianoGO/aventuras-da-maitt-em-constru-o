# Design Principles — Aventuras da Maittê

**Status:** DECIDED

These principles constrain later product, UX, narrative, pedagogical and technical decisions.

## 1. Learning is the game mechanic

Educational content must not be bolted onto an unrelated game. Challenges, progression and restoration of the world should make the act of learning consequential inside the adventure.

## 2. Curriculum before decoration

World design must be flexible enough to receive real curricular content. We do not invent educational content to justify a biome, puzzle or narrative scene. When content requirements conflict with scenery, curriculum requirements win.

## 3. Subject choice remains open

The player must be able to choose which subject-world to study from the shared origin/overworld. Completing World 1 must not be a prerequisite for entering World 2 or World 3 merely because of global progression.

Sequential locks are acceptable inside a subject-world when they reflect a valid learning progression or a deliberate local game progression.

## 4. Parametrization over hardcoding

Learning levels, words, numbers, operations, problem statements, examples and similar educational variables should be designed for future parametrization. Reusable challenge templates are preferred to one-off hardcoded exercises.

## 5. Child-facing UI should feel like part of the world

Navigation must be subtle and gamified rather than dominated by a conventional application header/menu. Detailed settings or administrative controls may exist on dedicated screens, but should not visually compete with the adventure during play.

## 6. Progress should be felt visually

The restoration of colors is a primary feedback and progression device. XP, unlocks and completion indicators may support it, but the world itself should visibly respond to learning.

## 7. Hope is the initial color anchor

The green heart on Maittê's shirt represents hope. It is the narrative anchor from which she pursues the other colors. This concept should remain coherent across story, progression and visual design.

## 8. Characters can be restored, not discarded

Minions and antagonistic characters should not default to elimination. When appropriate, learning/restoration can break their condition and allow them to return later as friendly or neutral characters in their world.

## 9. Biomes can inform characters, not curriculum

Minions may use animals and references from the habitat/biome of their world. This can strengthen world identity, but must not distort the educational objective of the challenge.

## 10. Maittê is inspired, not replicated

The protagonist should carry recognizable traits without becoming a realistic portrait. The target is a stylized line-art/storybook character with a light anime/manga influence, avoiding photorealism.

## 11. Personal companions support comprehension

Burpee, Pipoca, Will and Lyra can introduce or clarify challenge instructions in character. Their personalities should influence tone, but they should not solve the educational task for the player.

## 12. Feedback should support autonomy

The experience should encourage the child to try, understand and retry. Feedback must distinguish correct/incorrect outcomes without using punitive framing that makes mistakes feel like failure of the player.

## 13. Movement can be ambitious, delivery must be pragmatic

Dynamic character movement and richer animation are desirable, but the MVP must not depend on them. If Lovable cannot deliver a specific animation reliably, the interaction should degrade gracefully to simpler transitions rather than block the product.

## 14. Page-turning is the preferred major transition metaphor

For transitions between major contexts/screens, the current approved direction is a page-turning effect, consistent with the handcrafted/storybook language. Implementation details remain subject to usability and performance testing.

## 15. Build the system to expand

New regions, challenges and curricular content should be addable later without redesigning the fundamental navigation or rewriting each exercise flow.

## 16. Specs govern implementation

Lovable, Codex, Claude Code or any other implementation agent must follow the versioned specifications. Agents must not silently fill unresolved product gaps.
