# Product Vision — Aventuras da Maittê

**Status:** DECIDED

## Product purpose

Aventuras da Maittê is an educational adventure in which learning and progression are part of the same experience. The product must transform school-study content into challenges that make sense inside a coherent fictional universe, without turning the experience into a sequence of conventional worksheets with decorative game elements.

The application is initially conceived as a web system with strong tablet usability. A dedicated native app or game-engine implementation may be reconsidered later, but is not required for the MVP.

## Core experience

Maittê travels through a world whose colors have been stolen. Each subject is represented by a distinct world/region accessible from a shared origin/overworld. The player can choose which subject-world to study instead of being forced to finish one subject before opening another.

Progress inside each subject-world is sequential where pedagogically appropriate, but subject-worlds themselves are not globally chained. This preserves the freedom to study Mathematics, Portuguese, Science, History, Geography or English according to current study needs.

Learning restores color. The restoration is both narrative and systemic: completing challenges returns colors, expands the visual richness of the world and advances the story.

## Narrative premise

The main antagonist did not begin as an evil character. He gradually became gray after stopping learning and losing his curiosity. A spell led him to believe that if he could no longer see beauty in the world, nobody else should either, so he stole the colors.

The story must ultimately resolve through learning rather than punishment by destruction. As Maittê restores the colors, the spell is broken. The antagonist returns to school so that he can learn again.

Defeated minions are not disposable enemies. They can reappear after their conflict is resolved and may become inhabitants or characters of their respective worlds.

## Maittê as protagonist

The protagonist is inspired by Maittê without being a realistic portrait. Her visual language must remain stylized, predominantly line-based, with a light manga/anime influence and a handcrafted storybook sensibility.

Approved character elements include:

- hair slightly below the shoulders;
- a subtle bleached lock that may later receive different colors or be removed;
- frequent use of skirts;
- All Star-style sneakers;
- colorful socks;
- a green heart on the shirt, representing hope.

The antagonist steals the colors she progressively receives through XP. The green heart is conceptually important because hope is the initial reason she seeks the other colors.

## Companions

Maittê's pets are integrated into the adventure as recurring companions and can act as contextual narrators/helpers when a puzzle begins.

- Burpee — Border Collie blue merle, blue eyes; associated with intelligence and reasoning.
- Pipoca — white Maltese with slightly curly fur; adventurous and energetic.
- Will — orange mixed-breed cat; curious.
- Lyra — tricolor mixed-breed cat; observant.

They should appear throughout progression so that the adventure feels connected to Maittê's own world rather than functioning only as decorative mascots.

## Expansion model

The world structure must support future regions and additional content without requiring a redesign of the core navigation. New regions can be introduced in later updates.

Worlds should be designed to receive curricular content rather than having content invented merely to justify a pre-defined scenery. Content and difficulty must therefore be parametrizable.

## Product boundary for the MVP

The MVP starts with Mathematics. It does not need a game engine. The priority is validating the educational adventure loop, tablet-oriented interaction, world navigation, challenge system, progression and content parametrization in a web application.

## Non-goals of this document

This document does not define the final visual assets, exact world names, exact number of stages, complete curricular sequencing, backend technology or native-app migration strategy. Those belong to their respective specifications and ADRs.
